# 018 — The Full Timeline of How an AI Coding Darling Burned Its Most Loyal Users (Cursor pricing)
- **Source**: https://www.wearefounders.uk/cursors-pricing-disaster-the-full-timeline-of-how-an-ai-coding-darling-burned-its-most-loyal-users (We Are Founders, 2025-05 per metadata; text references May 2026 pricing)
- **Claim**: Cursor's flat-rate subscription collided with usage-scaled model costs, producing four pricing controversies (June 2025 plan change, auto-mode routing surprises, rate-limit changes, developer exodus) and repeated partial reversals. The author draws five lessons for usage-priced AI products.
- **Mechanism**: Cursor subsidised premium-model usage on a $20 Pro plan; as upstream models got costlier per token it moved to usage-based billing beyond an allocation and added rate caps. Auto mode routed requests to cheaper or premium models by judged complexity, so billing depended on system decisions. As of May 2026: Hobby free, Individual $20/month with nested Pro, Pro+, Ultra sub-tiers, Teams $40/user/month, Enterprise custom (pooled usage, invoice/PO billing).
- **Numbers**: Pro $20/month; Ultra $200 (earlier separate plan); Teams $40/user/month. Reported ARR from near zero to $100M in 12 months, then $200M+; valuation $50 billion (TechCrunch per author). Users posted daily charges of $10-$20; one thread said a team's $7,000 annual subscription was depleted in one day.
- **Token-cost implication**: Flat-rate seats over usage-metered models are fragile; builders should make routing and billing decisions visible, set spend limits, expect heavy users to dominate cost, and prefer direct pay-as-you-go (e.g. API) when predictability matters.
- **Caveats**: Secondary aggregation of Reddit, X and HN posts; author admits uncertainty on incident details (incident three mechanism is unclear); anecdotal figures are unverified; opinion-heavy.
- **Topic**: pricing
