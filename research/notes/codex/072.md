# 072 — Better prompt caching for GPT-6
- **Source**: https://openai.com/index/better-prompt-caching-for-gpt-6 (OpenAI, 2026-11)
- **Claim**: GPT-6 prompt caching expands eligible prefix reuse and adds dashboards, miss diagnostics, explicit breakpoints, and prewarming for persistent agents. OpenAI and named customers report that better cache structure materially reduced fresh-token processing and inference cost in production.
- **Mechanism**: Keep tool definitions, schemas, and ordering stable; append changing instructions near the end; use `allowed_tools` or `tool_choice: none` instead of deleting tool definitions; change effort through `configuration_update` without altering request-level effort; place explicit cache breakpoints around stable prefixes; prewarm known instructions and reference material; and diagnose misses by comparing model, tools, settings, and inputs against a recent response.
- **Numbers**: Cached input discounts are up to 90%, and eligible shared prefixes can be reused within a 30-minute window. GitHub Copilot reported more than a 50% reduction in the share of prompt tokens requiring fresh processing across billions of requests versus its prior baseline. One customer reported a few percentage points of hit-rate improvement and 20% lower cost; Manus reported roughly 85% to consistently above 90% cache hit rate in under a week; Wordsmith reported 83% to 91% hit rate in under a week, roughly two-thirds fewer cache writes, and 36% lower inference cost. The diagnostic example attributed 5,629 reusable but missed tokens to changed tools.
- **Token-cost implication**: Treat cache hit rate as a production metric with alerts, preserve stable prompt prefixes across tool and effort changes, and use explicit breakpoints plus prewarming for long-running or forked agents; validate savings from cached versus fresh token counts rather than assuming a cache-friendly layout works.
- **Caveats**: This is an OpenAI product announcement dated September 22, 2026 despite the corpus metadata month of 2026-11. The savings are OpenAI and customer self-reports without experimental designs, denominators, model-level prices, workload controls, or quality comparisons; “up to 90%” is a maximum discount, not a measured average.
- **Topic**: caching
