# 010 — Rerouting LLM Routers
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01818 (arXiv, 2025-01)
- **Claim**: Learned LLM routers are vulnerable to short, query-independent token sequences that force otherwise cheap-routed queries onto the strong model in both white-box and transfer settings. This can erase routing savings without materially degrading answer quality, and perplexity filtering can be optimized around.
- **Mechanism**: Prefix every query with a fixed-length confounder gadget. Generate it by randomized hill climbing: initialize tokens, repeatedly replace one random position with each of a sampled candidate set, retain the candidate maximizing the router’s strong-model score, and optionally add a penalty that aligns gadget perplexity with benign queries; for black-box attacks, optimize against a surrogate router and transfer the gadget.
- **Numbers**: contemporaneous prices per 1M input/output tokens: GPT-3.5-turbo $0.5/$1.5, GPT-4o-mini $0.15/$0.6, GPT-4o $2.5/$10, o1-preview $15/$60; routers calibrated to send 50% of workload to the strong model; gadget generation used batch size 32, 100 iterations, and 10 gadgets/router; white-box upgrade rates ranged from 73 ± 5% to 100 ± 0%, with worst final strong-routing rate 88 ± 2%; average convergence 58 iterations; Unify processing cost rose about 1.7×; OpenRouter rose from $0.03 to $0.25 for 72 MT-Bench queries, greater than 8×; perplexity defense at threshold 55 initially gave 3% false positives and 97% true positives, but low-perplexity optimization reduced average ROC-AUC to 0.66–0.71 on GSM8K while retaining substantial attack success.
- **Token-cost implication**: Treat router decisions as an adversarial cost-control surface: enforce per-user/per-tenant strong-model quotas, monitor strong-route frequency and cost drift, and do not rely on prompt complexity scores or perplexity filters alone.
- **Caveats**: The researchers designed and measured the attacks; open-source evaluation used 72 MT-Bench, 100 MMLU, and 100 GSM8K test queries per gadget, with routers calibrated on related distributions. Commercial router APIs were black boxes, Martian was excluded as apparently buggy, LLM-based filtering/paraphrasing was judged too costly rather than fully evaluated, and user-specific controls remain vulnerable to Sybil users.
