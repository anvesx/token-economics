# 031 — Mixture-of-Agents Enhances Large Language Model Capabilities
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04692 (Together AI, 2024-06)
- **Claim**: Layered aggregation of diverse LLM responses improved benchmark quality beyond any component model and surpassed GPT-4o on AlpacaEval 2.0, but it spends more calls and delays the first token until the final aggregation layer.
- **Mechanism**: In each layer, run several proposer models independently, concatenate their responses with the original prompt, and prompt the next layer’s agents to critically synthesize a better answer; reuse models across layers if desired and use one aggregator in the last layer. Select proposers for both quality and output diversity, because heterogeneous proposals beat repeated samples from one model, and select the final model for aggregation ability rather than standalone score.
- **Numbers**: Default MoA used 6 open-source proposers across 3 layers and Qwen1.5-110B-Chat as final aggregator; MoA scored 65.1 ± 0.6% length-controlled win rate and 59.8 ± 0.3% raw win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 versus GPT-4o at 57.5% and 51.3%. MoA with GPT-4o aggregator scored 65.7 ± 0.7%; MoA-Lite used 2 layers, scored 59.3 ± 0.2%, and beat GPT-4o by 1.8 percentage points. On MT-Bench, MoA scored 9.25 ± 0.10 and MoA with GPT-4o 9.40 ± 0.06. With 2 layers, 6 diverse proposers scored 61.3% versus 56.7% for 6 samples from one proposer and 47.8% for 1 proposer. The paper says MoA-Lite beat GPT-4 Turbo by about 4% while being more than 2× as cost-effective; pricing was retrieved May 22, 2024.
- **Token-cost implication**: Reserve multi-agent synthesis for tasks where quality justifies several full responses; parallelize proposers, use the smallest number of layers that reaches the target quality, and prefer diverse cheap proposers plus one strong aggregator over repeated calls to one model.
- **Caveats**: Together AI self-report using Together endpoints for open models; AlpacaEval, MT-Bench, and parts of FLASK use GPT-4-based judging, and the authors ran headline experiments three times. API-price estimates and TFLOPs are proxies, GPT-4 TFLOPs use a rumored 8×220B architecture, exact per-token prices are not listed, and iterative full-response aggregation raises time to first token. The paper notes conciseness was weaker and does not establish production latency or quality outside the tested benchmarks.
- **Topic**: agent-cost
