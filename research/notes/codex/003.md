# 003 — Hybrid LLM: Cost-Efficient and Quality-Aware Query Routing
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14618 (Microsoft Research, 2024-04)
- **Claim**: A learned two-model router can send queries on which a small model is likely to match a large model to the cheaper model, reducing large-model calls by up to 40% with no significant quality loss when the model pair is suitable. Modeling response randomness and correcting imbalanced training labels improves routing over a single-response classifier.
- **Mechanism**: Define the quality gap as BARTScore of the small-model response minus that of the large model. Fine-tune a DeBERTa-v3-large encoder to estimate either a hard one-sample label, the probability that the gap is nonnegative from 10 responses per model, or a relaxed probability that the gap exceeds `−t`; select `t` by grid-searching for maximum pairwise separation of transformed labels, then route scores above a calibrated threshold to the small model.
- **Numbers**: 20,000 MixInstruct examples: 10,000 train, 5,000 validation, 5,000 test; 10 responses per model/query for training; router size 300M parameters, trained 5 epochs on one NVIDIA A100 80GB; Llama-2 7B versus Llama-2 13B achieved 20% and 40% cost advantage with 0.0% quality drop using the transformed router; Llama-2 13B versus GPT-3.5-turbo achieved 20% cost advantage with 0.8% drop and 40% with 2.9% drop; router latency 0.036 ± 0.002 s/query versus 0.46 ± 0.039 s for FLAN-T5 800M, 7.99 ± 0.15 s for Llama-2 7B, and 14.61 ± 0.27 s for Llama-2 13B; thresholds calibrated on 500 validation samples; cross-pair generalization with correlations above 0.7 achieved 20% cost advantage with up to 1.6% drop and 40% with up to 4.1% drop.
- **Token-cost implication**: Route only after measuring per-query quality-gap separability for the exact model pair; calibrate the threshold against an allowed quality drop, and retrain when the pair or query distribution changes.
- **Caveats**: Cost advantage is the percentage of queries sent to the small model, not measured dollars, tokens, energy, or latency. The authors evaluate on a fixed model pair and data distribution, primarily train on BARTScore labels, assume encoder overhead is negligible, and leave N-model routing, load balancing, and out-of-distribution adaptation for future work.
