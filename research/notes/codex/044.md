# 044 — CO₂ Emissions and Models Performance: Insights from the Open LLM Leaderboard
- **Source**: https://huggingface.co/blog/leaderboard-emissions-analysis (Hugging Face, 2025-01)
- **Claim**: In Hugging Face’s fixed leaderboard setup, inference emissions generally rise with model size and output length, while benchmark-specific fine-tunes can emit less than base or official instruction models by terminating earlier and producing shorter, more coherent answers.
- **Mechanism**: Estimate CO₂ per evaluation from wall-clock evaluation time, cluster hardware power use, and electricity carbon intensity while holding hardware and evaluation method fixed; compare score, parameter count, and estimated emissions across model families, then inspect generative IFEval outputs to diagnose high-emission cases such as repetition, missing EOS behavior, and verbosity.
- **Numbers**: More than 3,000 models evaluated since June 2024; trend analysis retained 2,742 models, including 341 official-provider models. Community models below 10B parameters reached average leaderboard scores up to 35 (best scores around 45) for less than 5 kg CO₂. Qwen2.5 and Llama3.1 official 70B fine-tunes used about 2× the energy of their bases/community fine-tunes; for compact Llama3.1 and Qwen2.5, bases used about 2× the fine-tunes. IFEval max generation was 1,280 tokens; compared responses were 1,209 versus 1,444 characters, and repetitive examples reached 5,475 and 5,821 characters.
- **Token-cost implication**: Measure generated-token counts and termination behavior alongside quality; instruction tune or constrain EOS/max-output behavior so a model does not spend tokens continuing prompts, repeating text, or adding commentary that the task did not request.
- **Caveats**: Hugging Face observational analysis on one 8-GPU-per-node Transformers/Accelerate setup using spare cluster cycles; emissions are specific to that hardware, runtime, and electricity mix and are not general per-model inference footprints. Per-task CO₂ data were unavailable, model-family subsets required known parameter counts, generative and multiple-choice workloads differ, and the proposed verbosity explanation is exploratory rather than causal. Community fine-tunes may be benchmark-adapted or contaminated.
