# 035 — DeepSeek-V2: A Strong, Economical, and Efficient Mixture-of-Experts Language Model
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434 (DeepSeek AI, 2024-05)
- **Claim**: DeepSeek-V2 combines sparse expert activation with compressed latent attention to improve model quality while cutting both training compute and serving memory relative to DeepSeek 67B.
- **Mechanism**: Use DeepSeekMoE FFNs with fine-grained routed experts plus always-on shared experts, constrain each token’s experts to at most 3 devices, and add expert-, device-, and communication-balance losses plus capacity-based token dropping during training. Replace standard KV storage with Multi-head Latent Attention: jointly down-project each token’s keys and values into one latent vector, cache only that vector plus a small decoupled RoPE key, and absorb the up-projections into query/output matrices during inference; deploy with FP8 weights and an average 6-bit KV cache.
- **Numbers**: 236B total parameters, 21B activated per token, 60 layers, 128 attention heads, 2 shared plus 160 routed experts per MoE layer with 6 routed experts activated, 128K context, and 8.1T pretraining tokens. Versus DeepSeek 67B: 172.8K versus 300.6K H800 GPU-hours per 1T training tokens (42.5% lower), 93.3% less KV cache, and 5.76× maximum generation throughput. On one 8×H800 node, generation exceeded 50K tokens/s and prompt ingestion exceeded 100K tokens/s. MLA KV cache was 34.6K elements/token versus 860.2K for MHA in the large-MoE ablation (4% as much); long-context extension used 1,000 steps at 32K sequence length and batch 576, then tested through 128K.
- **Token-cost implication**: For self-hosted high-volume inference, optimize dollars per token by combining sparse activation with KV-cache compression; the cache reduction enables larger batches, while device-limited routing and load balancing keep expert communication from erasing the theoretical MoE savings.
- **Caveats**: DeepSeek’s own training and deployment measurements on H800 hardware and its internal HAI-LLM stack; no public dollar-per-token price, latency distribution, energy figure, or independent reproduction is provided. Comparisons are primarily with DeepSeek 67B and benchmark models under DeepSeek’s internal evaluation; FP8 and 6-bit cache quantization contribute to deployed results, so the gains are not attributable to MLA alone. The model is primarily Chinese/English and retains hallucination and stale-knowledge limits.
