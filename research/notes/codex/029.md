# 029 — Towards a Cascaded LLM Framework for Cost-effective Human-AI Decision-Making
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11887 (arXiv, 2025-06)
- **Claim**: A base-model → large-model → human cascade can jointly reduce compute, improve correctness, and abstain on uncertain cases; online threshold updates lowered cumulative risk versus single-model baselines on four of five evaluated datasets.
- **Mechanism**: Generate with the base model, estimate correctness with self-verification or YES/NO surrogate-token probability, calibrate the score with Bayesian logistic regression, accept when confidence exceeds a deferral threshold, otherwise regenerate with the large model, and abstain to a human when posterior uncertainty exceeds a model-specific threshold. Convert the hard decisions to sigmoid masks and update the deferral and abstention thresholds with ADAM on a differentiable risk combining error, expected model cost, and human-abstention cost; human-answered abstentions enter a replay buffer.
- **Numbers**: Main cascade Qwen-2.5-1.5B → Qwen-2.5-7B on 5 datasets (ARC2-Easy, ARC2-Challenge, MMLU, MedQA, MedMCQA) on one A100-class GPU; 100 calibration samples; cost proxy `s × (input tokens + 5 × output tokens)`; calibrated large-model STP n=1 ΔIBC was 242.7 ± 36.3% on ARC2-Easy, 89.3 ± 26.2% on ARC2-Challenge, 2.5 ± 5.2% on MMLU, 1.7 ± 16.3% on MedQA, and -10.0 ± 10.1% on MedMCQA. Online run: 1,000 unseen questions, ADAM learning rate 0.05, batch size 10, initial thresholds {0.5, 0.05, 0.05}, λc=10^-5, λa=0.1; cascade had lower cumulative regret on 4 of 5 datasets, not clearly on MedQA. Calibration-size ablation covered 50, 100, 200, and 500 samples with no significant performance change.
- **Token-cost implication**: Pay for a cheap verification pass before paying for large-model generation, calibrate confidence on local task data, and optimize thresholds against the real relative prices of generation and human review; do not deploy the cascade where confidence estimation shows no positive cost benefit.
- **Caveats**: Cambridge-authored, self-reported empirical paper; multiple-choice QA with binary correctness and Chain-of-Thought only, not open-ended production traffic. Cost is a parameter/token proxy rather than dollars or measured latency, output tokens are assigned a fixed 5× input cost, human feedback is assumed available after abstention, noisy labels increase regret, and MedQA did not beat the single-model approach. Results depend on initialization, λ weights, calibration quality, and the selected open models.
- **Topic**: routing
