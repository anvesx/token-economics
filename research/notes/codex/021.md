# 021 — FrugalGPT: How to Use Large Language Models While Reducing Cost and Improving Performance
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176 (Stanford University, 2023-05)
- **Claim**: Heterogeneous LLM prices and complementary errors make prompt adaptation, approximation, and model cascades viable cost controls. A learned three-model cascade matched the best single model with 59.2–98.3% lower measured API cost and sometimes improved accuracy at the same or lower cost.
- **Mechanism**: For cascades, train a lightweight response-scoring regressor, choose an ordered list of models and per-stage acceptance thresholds under an average budget, call models from cheap to expensive, and stop when a response’s reliability score clears the stage threshold. The optimizer prunes model orders with low answer disagreement and interpolates objective values; the paper also proposes shorter prompt selection, batched query concatenation, completion caching, and distilling expensive outputs into cheaper models.
- **Numbers**: 12 APIs from 5 providers; March 2023 cost for 10M input/output tokens: GPT-4 $30/$60, GPT-3 $20/$20, ChatGPT $2/$2, GPT-J $0.2/$5; customer-service example: 360,000 queries/month, 1,800 input and 80 output tokens/query, about $21.2K/month on GPT-4; datasets: HEADLINES 10,000, OVERRULING 2,400, COQA 7,982; cascade length 3; equal-accuracy cost: HEADLINES $33.1 to $0.6, 98.3% saving; OVERRULING $9.7 to $2.6, 73.3%; COQA $72.5 to $29.6, 59.2%; HEADLINES case budget $6.5 versus GPT-4 $33.1, accuracy 0.872 versus 0.857; maximum reported accuracy improvement over GPT-4 was 4% in the abstract and up to 5% in the Figure 5 discussion.
- **Token-cost implication**: Price input, output, and per-request charges separately; route easy queries through cheap models first, but include judge calls and fall-through calls in the budget, and use caching or prompt sharing when repeated context dominates input tokens.
- **Caveats**: The authors evaluated prices captured in March 2023 and three datasets; savings are workload- and price-dependent. The cascade requires labeled, distribution-matched training examples and an upfront training/evaluation cost, may call multiple models per query, and does not optimize latency, fairness, privacy, or environmental impact.
