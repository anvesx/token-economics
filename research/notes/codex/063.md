# 063 — Inference Auctions
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40070 (arXiv, 2026-09)
- **Claim**: An auction can allocate low-latency inference priority according to users’ bids while preserving the KV-cache efficiency of prefix-aware scheduling. In experiments, the proposed schedule improved economic welfare over bid-blind cache-optimal ordering without materially changing cache hit rate or mean time-to-first-token.
- **Mechanism**: Represent queued prompts as a radix tree and restrict service order to depth-first traversals, which keep shared-prefix subtrees contiguous. At every node, sort child subtrees by descending total bid divided by request count; pair this welfare-maximizing DFS order with VCG payments computed from each user’s local externalities, and pace repeated-auction bids by scaling request value with a budget multiplier updated by gradient descent.
- **Numbers**: Scheduling runs in expected O(M + n log ν) for M total prompt tokens, n requests, and maximum tree branching ν; all VCG payments run in O(min(M, mn) log ν) for m users. Semi-synthetic tests used a single 80GB H100, a 32B model, queue Q=1,024, a 23,000-token KV cache, at most 128 decoded tokens/query, and three trials. Versus bid-blind DFS, bid-aware DFS improved welfare by 12–18.6% for i.i.d. values and up to 100% for non-i.i.d. values, while retaining about 80% of unconstrained bid-sort welfare; unconstrained sorting increased mean latency by as much as 12×. At n=1,024 it was 2.4× faster than bid-sort on chains and 12× on long trees; fast payments took 34 ms versus 370 ms naively and agreed within 10^-12. The autobidder has O(sqrt(T)) regret; in T=60 batches of n=256, paced welfare averaged 0.91 of the truthful DFS optimum on stars and 0.93 on chains.
- **Token-cost implication**: When serving repeated or agentic prefixes under load, schedule entire prefix-sharing subtrees together and express urgency by ranking subtrees rather than individual requests; this can monetize priority without paying the token recomputation and latency penalty caused by cache-thrashing bid order. Release large batches under saturation, but add a timeout under light load because batching wait dominated at 1 query/second.
- **Caveats**: This is an author-run prototype on semi-synthetic SQuAD, GSM8K, SWE-agent, and tree-of-thought workloads, with synthetic Pareto values and truthful bids; every reported number is a mean of three trials. The mechanism handles one inference worker and assumes the KV cache can hold the longest request; it does not solve multi-worker routing, richer latency preferences, batch formation, or collusion, and welfare is an economic objective rather than dollar cost or answer quality.
