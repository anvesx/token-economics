# 136 — Exploring the Trade-Offs: Quantization Methods, Task Difficulty, and Model Size in LLMs From Edge to Giant
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11055 (arXiv v6, 4 Jun 2025; Lee, Park, Kwon, Oh, Kwon; ETRI, KETI, Neubla)
- **Claim**: Quantized instruction-tuned LLMs generally beat smaller FP16 models across 13 datasets, but lose on instruction-following (IFEval) and hallucination (TruthfulQA). FP8 is the most robust method; AWQ beats GPTQ in weight-only 4-bit; 4-bit hurts small models badly but 70B-scale models stay stable.
- **Mechanism**: Post-training quantization of Vicuna, Gemma and Llama (1B to 405B, 12 models) with GPTQ (AutoGPTQ, llmcompressor, AutoRound; 4-bit, group size 128), AWQ (AutoAWQ, 4-bit, group size 128), SmoothQuant (W8A8, llmcompressor) and FP8 E4M3 (weights and activations, per tensor, llmcompressor). Evaluated with lm-eval on OpenLLM Leaderboard v1 and v2 tasks plus MT-Bench (GPT-4 judge), greedy decoding, vLLM 0.6.6.post1 on a four-server cluster (H100 x8, A100 x4, RTX 6000 x4, A6000 x4).
- **Numbers**: Storage (GB): Llama-3.1-405B FP16 810, FP8 405, 4-bit 202.5; 70B FP16 140, FP8 70, 4-bit 35; Llama-2-7B FP16 14, 4-bit 3.5. Examples: 4-bit Llama-2-13B (6.5 GB) beats FP16 Llama-2-7B (14 GB) on most tasks; 4-bit Llama-3.1-405B (202.5 GB) beats FP16 Llama-3.1-70B (140 GB) on most tasks but not IFEval. Llama-3.1-70B leaderboard-v1 average: FP16 78.76, FP8 78.55, AWQ 78.70, GPTQ (AutoGPTQ) 78.51. Llama-3.1-405B v1 average: FP16 81.89, FP8 82.19, AWQ 82.20, GPTQ (llmcompressor) 81.76, SmoothQuant 79.45; v2 average: FP16 47.70, FP8 46.42, GPTQ 45.91, AWQ 42.66, SmoothQuant 35.56 (SmoothQuant up to 10.86% below FP8 on v2, text). Llama-3.2-1B GPTQ 4-bit (AutoRound): GSM8k -25.32 points, IFEval -16.01 points; v1 average 47.81 to 37.67. Llama-3.2-3B with SmoothQuant gains 13.32% (v1) and 8.72% (v2) over FP16 Llama-3.2-1B. Llama-3.1-8B: GPTQ 8-bit beats 4-bit by +2.51% (v1) and +2.11% (v2) on average; at 70B, 4-bit beats 8-bit by +1.89%. SLM (1B, 3B) average drop under SmoothQuant is below 1%. FP8 on weights and activations halves KV cache size (FP8 KV cache supported by vLLM). MT-Bench: Reasoning improved after quantization for 9 of 12 models (about +1 point in one analysis); Coding, Humanities and Extraction most often among top-3 losses (16, 12, 10 of 24 configurations); Llama-3.3-70B avg 9.13 FP16, 9.09 GPTQ, 8.94 AWQ (turn-averaged 9.13/9.04/8.94); Llama-3.1-405B turn-averaged 9.12/9.10/9.14. MT-Bench GPT-4 judging cost $4 to $7 per model, about $200 total. Run time above 30 days prevented testing all Llama-3.1-405B GPTQ configurations.
- **Token-cost implication**: Weight compression cuts memory 2x (FP8) or 4x (4-bit) and so GPUs per replica. FP8 gives near-lossless quality at half the memory with hardware speed benefit on H100 and RTX 6000 Ada, so it is the default choice; use AWQ over GPTQ for 4-bit; do not use 4-bit on 1B to 3B models without testing; a larger quantized model can replace a smaller FP16 model at similar memory. Re-test instruction-following and hallucination metrics and coding/STEM tasks before shipping a quantized model.
- **Caveats**: Quality measurements only; no latency, throughput or dollar figures. Table layouts in the text are partly garbled; model-to-row mapping is from table order. Different GPTQ implementations give different results. MT-Bench judged by GPT-4, which sometimes marks wrong answers correct; Llama-3.3-70B categories near 10 make differences hard to see. Reasoning gains may reflect shorter answers rewarded by the judge. Results are for Llama, Vicuna and Gemma instruction-tuned models only; 405B SmoothQuant failure is attributed to its design for models up to OPT-175B (authors' hypothesis).
- **Topic**: quantization
