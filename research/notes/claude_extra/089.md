# 089 — Do NOT Think That Much for 2+3=? On the Overthinking of o1-Like LLMs
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21187 (Tencent AI Lab / Shanghai Jiao Tong University, arXiv, 2024-12)
- **Claim**: o1-like reasoning models (QwQ-32B-Preview, DeepSeek-R1 family) spend far more tokens than needed on easy problems, mostly on later solution rounds that add little accuracy or diversity. Self-training on shortened responses cuts tokens substantially while keeping accuracy roughly flat.
- **Mechanism**: Two metrics. Outcome efficiency = (1/N) sum of sigma_i * T-hat_i / T_i, where T_i is total tokens and T-hat_i is tokens up to the first correct answer (sigma_i = 1 if correct). Process efficiency = (1/N) sum of D_i / T_i, where D_i counts tokens in solutions that add a new reasoning strategy (GPT-4o clusters solutions by strategy). Mitigation is self-training on PRM12K: sample 10 responses per problem at temperature 1.0, keep correct ones, and build training data. Options: SFT on the shortest response; DPO, RPO and SimPO with the shortest as positive and the longest sample as negative (better than greedy as negative). Response simplification variants: First-Correct Solution (FCS), FCS+Reflection (keeps the first correct solution plus a second one), Greedily Diverse Solutions (GDS, adds only solutions with new strategies). Best recipe: SimPO with FCS+Reflection positives.
- **Numbers**: On "2 plus 3", o1-like models used 1,953% more tokens than conventional models. In over 92% of cases the first solution round is already correct, and it is under 60% of total tokens (QwQ on ASDIV: first round 287 tokens, 38.7% of the response). Worked example (2+3): 13 solutions, 901-token response, first solution 39 tokens, process efficiency 35.8%. Table 4, MATH500, QwQ-32B-Preview baseline: accuracy 93.0, 3.2 solutions, 2407.9 tokens, outcome efficiency 52.3%, process efficiency 71.2%. SimPO with shortest response: 91.6 accuracy, 1871.8 tokens, 67.6%/80.9% (22.3% fewer tokens). SimPO with FCS+Reflection: 92.8 accuracy, 1.9 solutions, 1330.7 tokens, 80.0%/89.5%. FCS alone: 91.0 accuracy, 1016.0 tokens, 88.7%/98.1%. GSM8K: 96.9 to 96.8 accuracy, 741.8 to 381.6 tokens. ASDIV: 94.8 to 96.0 accuracy, 772.8 to 416.6 tokens. GPQA: 59.6 to 59.1 accuracy, 3228.4 to 2085.7 tokens. AIME24: 46.7 to 43.3 accuracy, 9480.9 to 5154.5 tokens. Conventional Qwen2.5-Math-72B-Instruct comparison: GPQA 46.5 accuracy, 811.7 tokens; AIME24 23.3 accuracy, 1204.5 tokens.
- **Token-cost implication**: Do not route easy questions to long-reasoning models; most of their extra tokens come after the first correct answer. If you can post-train or distill, train on first-correct-plus-one-reflection traces with a preference loss to cut reasoning tokens by roughly 30 to 45 percent on the reported sets. Also measure first-correct-token share on your own traffic to size the waste.
- **Caveats**: Self-reported by the authors. Tested mainly on QwQ-32B-Preview for training (the only open model available to post-train); math and GPQA only; no dollar prices. AIME24 accuracy dropped 46.7 to 43.3 and MATH500 dropped 93.0 to 92.8 with FCS+Reflection. FCS alone lost accuracy on hard MATH500. Efficiency metrics rely on GPT-4o solution clustering.
