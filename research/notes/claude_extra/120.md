# 120 — CacheBlend: Fast Large Language Model Serving for RAG with Cached Knowledge Fusion
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16444 (arXiv; Yao, Li, Liu, Ray, Cheng, Zhang, Du, Lu, Jiang; Univ. of Chicago, Stanford, Microsoft Research; EuroSys '25; v3 3 Apr 2025; 2024-05)
- **Claim**: CacheBlend reuses precomputed KV caches of RAG chunks even when they are not the prompt prefix, and recomputes KV for only a small fraction of tokens to restore cross-chunk attention. Versus full KV recompute it cuts time-to-first-token (TTFT) 2.2-3.3x and raises throughput 2.8-5x with negligible quality loss.
- **Mechanism**: Prefix caching only saves the first chunk; full KV reuse (PromptCache) concatenates per-chunk caches with fixed positions but ignores cross-attention, so quality drops. CacheBlend: (1) fetches each chunk's stored KV (hashed like vLLM blocks, LRU eviction, one storage tier such as CPU RAM or SSD) and fixes RoPE positions by a rotation of the K vectors; (2) prefills layer by layer, and on each layer recomputes KV only for "high-KV-deviation" (HKVD) tokens, those whose precomputed KV differs most from a full recompute. Because HKVD tokens correlate across neighbouring layers (Spearman rank correlation), it recomputes all tokens on layer 1, keeps the top r1% by deviation, recomputes those on layer 2 and keeps a slightly smaller r2%, and so on (gradual filtering) to average r% per layer; (3) pipelines: recompute of layer i overlaps with loading layer i+1 KV from storage, with two threads and a synchronize() before each layer. A loading controller picks the recompute ratio r so that recompute time equals KV load time, floored at r* = 15% (the empirical minimum for quality), and picks the cheapest storage device whose load delay does not exceed recompute time. Recompute cost is r% of a full prefill. Implemented in about 3K lines of Python on vLLM (PyTorch 2.0); open source in LMCache.
- **Numbers**: TTFT reduction 2.2-3.3x vs full KV recompute across 3 models and 4 datasets; throughput up to 5x vs full recompute and 3.3x vs prefix caching (2.8-5x stated overall); F1 or Rouge-L drop within 0.02 (text also says 0.01-0.03) vs full recompute and prefix caching; 0.15-0.35 better F1/Rouge-L than full KV reuse; vs full KV reuse at almost the same TTFT, 0.1-0.2 higher absolute F1 (QA) and 0.03-0.25 higher Rouge-L (summarization). Update fraction below 15% typically suffices; 5-18% recompute ratio gives at most 0.002 quality loss on Yi-34B, equal to 4.1-6.6x TTFT reduction vs full recompute and 3.4-6.1x vs prefix caching. Vs MapReduce RAG: 2-5x lower TTFT with higher F1. Per-layer example, Llama-7B with a 4K context: recompute 15% of tokens takes 3 ms, loading one layer's KV from NVMe SSD takes 16 ms (recompute hidden). Llama-70B: recompute 7 ms vs load 4 ms (not fully hidden). Baseline prefill: a 4K-token input takes about 3 s (Llama-34B) or 6 s (Llama-70B) on one A40. Hash table is 16 MB per one million chunks. Setup: Mistral-7B, Yi-34B (8-bit), Llama-70B (8-bit); Runpod with 2x A40, 128 GB RAM, 1 TB NVMe at 4.8 GB/s; datasets 2WikiMQA (200 cases), Musique (150), SAMSum (200), MultiNews (60); 512-token chunks, top-6 chunks by L2 distance; extended datasets of 6,000 queries each (1,500 original + 4,500 GPT-4-generated similar queries), skipping the first 1K queries to warm the cache. No dollar prices.
- **Token-cost implication**: In RAG with a reused chunk store, prefix caching saves little because only the first chunk is a prefix. If you self-host (vLLM, LMCache), blend chunk caches and recompute about 15% of tokens to cut prefill compute and TTFT by 2-3x while keeping quality. Store KV on cheap SSD if the load time hides the recompute. Use a controller to pick the recompute ratio and storage tier. Skip MapReduce and MapRerank for multi-chunk questions: slower or lower quality. It lowers GPU time per request, not per-token API prices, and does nothing for hosted APIs that you cannot patch.
- **Caveats**: Authors' own benchmarks. Only open models up to 70B and only 8-bit quantized for 34B and 70B. Transformers only (Mamba and Griffin untested). Baseline prefix caching was given an idealized zero loading delay. Quality measured by F1 and Rouge-L on small test sets (60-200 cases) with synthetic query extensions. Single-node, vLLM only; not tested on DistServe or Sarathi or across nodes. Single storage tier. Gains depend on high chunk reuse and on chunk caches staying in storage. Speedups shrink on slower storage (loading dominates).
