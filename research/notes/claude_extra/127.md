# 127 — AI Energy Score v2: Refreshed Leaderboard, now with Reasoning
- **Source**: https://huggingface.co/blog/sasha/ai-energy-score-v2 (Hugging Face community blog; Sasha Luccioni, Boris Gamazaychikov; 2025-12-04)
- **Claim**: The AI Energy Score leaderboard is refreshed with 39 new models and a reasoning task. Reasoning models use about 30x more GPU energy on average than non-reasoning ones, and 150x to 700x more when the same model has reasoning switched on.
- **Mechanism**: Open-source AI Energy Benchmarks package (built with Scott Chamberlin of Neuralwatt) uses Code Carbon and the same custom datasets as the Feb 2025 v1 leaderboard, run in a Docker-based process on standardized recent GPUs. Energy is reported as GPU Wh per 1,000 queries. Models are grouped in Class A (fit one consumer GPU), B (one cloud GPU) and C (multiple GPUs). The efficiency-progress check compares non-reasoning, non-MoE models against a Feb 2025 reference model of similar active-parameter size.
- **Numbers**: GPU Wh per 1k queries, reasoning off vs on: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B 49.53 vs 7,626.53 (154x); Phi-4-reasoning-plus 15B 18.42 vs 9,461.61 (514x); SmolLM3-3B 18.35 vs 12,791.22 (697x). Reasoning-on models used 300 to 800 times more output tokens than base equivalents. GPT-OSS: the 20B class swings 4.8x between high and low reasoning modes, the 120B class 1.6x; across classes the low setting differs 4.7x and medium/high about 1.6x. 39 new models added, 21 of them text generation (11 Class A, 3 Class B, 7 Class C). Of 14 comparable newer models, 8 used equal or more energy than a similar-size Feb 2025 model; the range was from 3% of the energy to almost 2x. No dollar prices.
- **Token-cost implication**: Reasoning tokens drive cost and energy; do not enable reasoning by default. Gate it per task, use low or medium effort settings where offered, and route simple queries to a smaller or non-reasoning model. Output-token count, not parameter count, becomes the main cost predictor for reasoning models, so budget and monitor output tokens per request. Newer models are not automatically cheaper, so re-benchmark when swapping. The authors say energy data can feed a router alongside quality metrics.
- **Caveats**: Energy only (GPU), not dollars or latency; Hugging Face's own benchmark, mostly open-weight models, on their custom datasets and hardware. Only three reasoning model pairs are shown in the table. Proprietary models are mostly not covered. Blog post, not peer reviewed; it did not test agentic, coding or tool-use tasks. The comparison used a small set of 14 models.
