# 083 — Selective Context: Compressing Context to Enhance Inference Efficiency of Large Language Models
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06201 (University of Surrey / University of Manchester, arXiv, 2023-10)
- **Claim**: Pruning low-information lexical units from the input context, using self-information from a small causal LM, halves context size with a small quality drop and lowers GPU memory and per-token latency.
- **Mechanism**: A base causal LM (LLaMA-7B for open models, GPT-3 curie for OpenAI models) computes self-information I(x) = -log2 P(x_t | x_<t) per token, sentence by sentence (because later tokens get lower self-information if the whole context is scored at once). Tokens are merged into lexical units (tokens, noun phrases via spaCy, or sentences via NLTK) by summing self-information. Units with self-information at or above the p-th percentile are kept (percentile filtering, not a fixed top-k). Phrase level was best; sentence level was unstable. Reference outputs are the model's answers on the full context.
- **Numbers**: Abstract: 50% context reduction gives 36% less inference memory and 32% less inference time, with a drop of .023 BERTScore and .038 faithfulness over four tasks. Table 1 (avg across models, temp 0.7), drop vs original at ratios 0.2/0.35/0.5/0.65/0.8: BLEU .05/.10/.17/.22/.28 (original .347); ROUGE-1 .03/.07/.12/.18/.26 (original .571); BERTScore F1 drop .007/.013/.023/.032/.047 (original .909). Table 2 (greedy), Selective Context vs random deletion at 0.5: BERTScore F1 .900 vs .873, ROUGE-1 .642 vs .576. Faithfulness (gpt-3.5-turbo, 200 QA per ratio): unfaithful tuple rate .027/.050/.038/.051/.086 at 0.2/0.35/0.5/0.65/0.8; "Sorry" refusals 0/6/4/19/27; answer length 160.3 tokens full vs 131.2 at 0.65. Case study at ratio 0.5 on vicuna-13b: CUDA memory 77,695 MB to 61,885 MB, 110.8 to 76.3 ms/token (1.32x faster), 46.1 ms one-time to build the selective context. Datasets: 408 arXiv, 470 ShareGPT, 294 BBC documents (all under 2048 tokens), created after March 2023.
- **Token-cost implication**: A cheap, model-agnostic pre-filter can cut input tokens about 20-35% with near-baseline output similarity; beyond 50% quality and refusals rise. If you pay per input token, compression at ratio 0.35-0.5 reduces billed tokens; weigh against caching, since pruned text changes the prefix. Run the scorer on a small local model.
- **Caveats**: Self-reported by the authors. Quality is measured as similarity to the model's own full-context answers (BLEU/ROUGE/BERTScore), not task accuracy against gold labels. Contexts are short (under 2048 tokens); models are 2023-era (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, LLaMA, Vicuna). Memory and latency measured on a single example. Phrase boundaries rely on spaCy noun chunks; the best percentile varies by task and was not auto-tuned. Question-agnostic. LLaMA models often failed to follow instructions on compressed context.
- **Topic**: compression
