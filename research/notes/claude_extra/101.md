# 101 — Accelerating Large Language Model Inference with Self-Supervised Early Exits (Dynamic Self-Speculative Decoding)
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21082 (arXiv, 2024-07, v3 dated 10 Jul 2026; author Florian Valade, Universite Gustave Eiffel / Fujitsu)
- **Claim**: Small early-exit heads attached to a frozen LLM, with a per-head confidence threshold calibrated on held-out data, let easy tokens exit at shallow layers. Used as the draft stage of self-speculative decoding (DSSD), it needs one accuracy threshold instead of a tuned head and draft length, and reaches higher draft acceptance than LayerSkip.
- **Mechanism**: K = 4 early-exit heads (MLPs like the final head) are placed at layers floor(k*L/(K+1)). The backbone stays frozen. Heads are trained on MiniPile with a self-supervised KL loss to the main model's output (500,000 examples, learning rate 5e-5); randomly initialized heads beat heads copied from the lm head because copied heads are overconfident. Entropy was the best confidence metric by ROC AUC across six Pythia sizes. Calibration: on a held-out set, for each head find the smallest entropy-score threshold such that the share of tokens above it that agree with the full model's argmax is at least epsilon. Inference: exit at the first head whose confidence passes its threshold; otherwise use the full model. DSSD: draft tokens with the first confident head from shallow to deep, into a buffer (limit e.g. 32 tokens, stop on EOS or when no head is confident), then verify the whole buffer in one full-model forward pass and keep tokens up to the first mismatch.
- **Numbers**: Pythia-2.8B (V100 32GB, table of epsilon sweep): epsilon 0.90 gives 12.0% speedup, 0.80 gives 20.2%, 0.70 gives 30.3%, 0.50 gives 58.6% (speedup is measured as reduction in mean output layer). Pythia-2.8B accuracy at epsilon 0.5 vs baseline: PIQA 0.615 vs 0.742, SciQ 0.436 vs 0.882, LAMBADA 0.249 vs 0.637; Winogrande 0.541 vs 0.571 (no loss). At epsilon 0.9: PIQA 0.730, SciQ 0.867, LAMBADA 0.630. Text says moderate epsilon gives 10% to 20% speedup depending on model size. Speedup grows with model size: at epsilon 0.5, Pythia-70M 24.0%, 410M 49.2%, 1.4B 55.0%, 2.8B 58.6%. DSSD on Pythia-2.8B, 200-token greedy decoding: acceptance 88.8% at epsilon 0.9 vs best LayerSkip 53.6% (1.66x); discarded draft tokens 8 vs 128 (text also says 9 vs 128 in another place); mean exit layer 13.6 vs 24.0 for the full model. LayerSkip baseline was a 24-configuration grid (4 heads x 6 speculation lengths).
- **Token-cost implication**: This is a model-internals technique for people who host and modify their own models. It lowers compute per token, not token count, and does nothing for API pricing. If you self-host and use self-speculative decoding, calibrate one threshold instead of grid-searching head and draft length, and expect larger gains on bigger models.
- **Caveats**: Single author, preprint, only Pythia 70M to 2.8B (old small models, no modern instruction-tuned LLMs). Speedup is a layer-count proxy (mean output layer), not measured wall-clock latency or throughput; no tokens per second reported. Plain early exit loses real accuracy at low epsilon. DSSD results are on one model with prompts from the authors' own dataset and greedy decoding; "acceptance rate" is the main metric, not end-to-end speed. Authors say they could not test larger models. KV-cache handling for skipped layers is not discussed in the text.
