# 273 — Hosting Qwen on Blackwell (Perplexity)
- **Source**: https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/hosting-qwen-on-blackwell (Perplexity, 2026-03; citation date 2026-05-12)
- **Claim**: Perplexity describes serving its post-trained Qwen3 235B MoE on NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 racks with an in-house engine, and says this handles more traffic at lower cost without losing accuracy versus its Hopper deployments.
- **Mechanism**: Prefill is disaggregated from decode. Prefillers use tensor parallelism across 4 GPUs of one node (TP=4, EP=4) with an all-reduce over NVLink plus NVSwitch SHARP; the number of prefiller replicas is scaled to match total input tokens received to avoid queuing. Decoders use only data parallelism plus expert parallelism, each device keeping its own KV cache, sharded across up to 16 devices over the 72-way NVLink domain; decoder count is scaled to keep per-decoder batch size under a threshold. KV caches move prefiller to decoder over InfiniBand via a TransferEngine, laid out HND so shards can be split or concatenated in flight. In-house CuTeDSL dispatch/combine and all-reduce kernels, CUTLASS MoE GEMM, MTP-layer speculative decoding, and weights statically converted to MXFP8 after fine-tuning.
- **Numbers**: GB200 NVL72: 18 nodes, 72 GPUs, 180GB HBM each, 1800 GB/s NVLink between any two peers, 400 Gb/s ConnectX-7 InfiniBand. Qwen3 235B: 128 experts, 8 routed per token, 4 KV heads; ~6000-token input gives ~375 tokens per expert. SHARP: theoretical traffic cut 33% with 4 peers, observed about 10% lower prefill and decode latency on H200 and GB200. Decoder sharding across 16 ranks justified because about 5 us extra dispatch/combine latency (EP=8 to EP=16) is outweighed by more than 10 us less GEMM cost. Decode benchmark assumed draft acceptance length 0.8. No dollar costs, tokens/sec, or traffic-share figures given in the text.
- **Token-cost implication**: If you self-host a large MoE, separate prefill and decode and size each pool on its own metric (input tokens/s for prefill, per-decoder batch size for decode). Prefer a large NVLink domain for decode, and use MXFP8 where microscaling hardware exists. This only matters at serving scale; API buyers can use it to judge why providers on newer hardware can price lower.
- **Caveats**: Self-reported by Perplexity, Qwen3 235B only; the cost claim is qualitative and no absolute throughput or $/token numbers appear in the text. MXFP4/NVFP4 were rejected for accuracy loss without quantization-aware training, and untested further. Hopper comparison used block scaling on both to isolate parallelism.
