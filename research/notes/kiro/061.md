# 061 — Tiered Super-Moore's Law: Price Evolution, Production Frontiers, and Market Competition in LLM Inference Services
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28576 (arXiv, 2026-03)
- **Claim**: First systematic economic analysis of LLM token pricing; documents a ~600-fold token price decline (2020–2026) and proposes a "Tiered Super-Moore" hypothesis: economy and mid-tier prices fall faster than Moore's Law, while flagship prices defy exponential decay due to a reasoning premium.
- **Mechanism**: Assembles 3 datasets — OpenRouter API cross-section (318 models, collected 2026-03-28), Epoch AI panel (3,237 models, 2020–2026), and 62 cross-validated milestone pricing records. Fits per-tier exponential decay P(t)=P0·e^(−λt), half-life = ln2/λ. Chow test for structural break in pooled log-price. DEA (CCR model, blended price input, 0–100 quality output) for efficiency θ; Malmquist Productivity Index = efficiency change (EC) × technical change (TC). OLS/panel regression of ln(inference price) on ln(training cost). Tiers: Flagship >$5/M input; Mid $0.5–$5/M; Economy <$0.5/M. Core causal mechanism argued: software/architectural innovation (MoE cutting active params 671B→37B ≈18×, Flash Attention, KV cache, speculative decoding), not hardware, drives the decline.
- **Numbers**: ~600-fold decline: GPT-3 $60/M (Jun 2020) → Gemini 2.0 Flash $0.10/M (Feb 2025). Input-price range $0.01/M (Liquid) to $150/M (o1-pro) = 15,000×. Half-lives: economy 1.10 yr (82% / 1.82× faster than Moore's 2-yr), mid 1.55 yr (29% / 1.29× faster), flagship 3.59 yr (R²=0.031, inapplicable), pooled 1.32 yr. R²: economy 0.192, mid 0.307, flagship 0.031. Reasoning premium avg 31.5× (2024Q3 83.3×, 2024Q4 18.8×, 2025Q1 0.51× [DeepSeek-R1 $0.55/M vs o1 $15/M, 96% discount], 2025Q2 23.4×). Chow break 2024-05-01 (F=5.736, p=0.005; 16 pre / 44 post); secondary 2024-07-01 (F=5.29, p=0.008). TFP residual ≈103.7% of cost reduction; GPU contribution ≈ −0.9%. MPI peak 4.107 (2024Q1→Q4), TC=4.133. Only 2 of 318 models on DEA frontier (both Liquid $0.01/M); mean θ=0.087 (typical model priced 11.5× frontier); 88.7% have θ<0.2. Vendor θ: Liquid 0.778, Meta 0.258, NVIDIA 0.146, Google 0.134, Qwen 0.109, Mistral 0.105, OpenAI 0.115, Anthropic 0.039. Training→inference elasticity β=0.432 (SE 0.204, p=0.050; HC3 p=0.071; R²=0.219, n=18). US–China training-cost gap 63× (GPT-4.5 $340M vs DeepSeek-V3 $5.4M) but $/FLOP difference insignificant (Welch t=1.25, p=0.228; China 2.41e−18, US 2.25e−18); compute gap 115× (3.8e26 vs 3.3e24 FLOP). HHI fell 4,558→2,086 (−54.2%); OpenAI share 65%→34%; DeepSeek 0→13% in 18 months; CR4 ~95%→81%. Cumulative 2023Q1–2025Q4: 99.3% cost saving at equal quality, 46.7% performance gain at equal price. GPT-4.5 launched $75/$150 per 1M in/out; o3-mini $1.10/$4.40; Claude 3.7 Sonnet $3/$15.
- **Token-cost implication**: Builders should expect economy/mid-tier prices to roughly halve every ~1.1–1.6 years, so defaulting to economy-tier models captures most of the ongoing deflation; frontier/reasoning models carry a ~31.5× premium that is market power (an innovation rent), not cost, and gets disciplined fast by open-source entrants — so treat flagship pricing as negotiable/temporary and use open-weights models as a price ceiling. Most models are priced ~11.5× above the efficiency frontier, implying large savings by shopping the price–quality frontier (e.g. OpenRouter).
- **Caveats**: Single-author working paper (arXiv v1, not peer-reviewed). Small milestone sample (62 obs; training regression n=18) constrains precision. No transaction-volume data — market shares are proxy-based, so HHI is estimated. Quality score is a heuristic 0–100 composite. Six-year window in a fast-moving market; relationships may shift. Cross-section is a single snapshot (2026-03-28).
- **Topic**: pricing
