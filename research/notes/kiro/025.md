# 025 — DistillSpec: Improving speculative decoding via knowledge distillation
- **Source**: https://research.google/pubs/distillspec-improving-speculative-decoding-via-knowledge-distillation (Google Research, 2023-12; published ICLR 2024)
- **Claim**: DistillSpec uses white-box knowledge distillation to better align a small draft model with the large target model, improving speculative decoding and yielding a ~20% speedup over standard speculative decoding across five tasks.
- **Mechanism**: Speculative decoding speeds inference by having a small draft model generate tokens and the large target model verify them in parallel. DistillSpec distills the target model into the draft model to raise acceptance rates. Two key findings for successful distillation: (1) on-policy data generation (training on sequences the draft model itself generates) and (2) choosing a divergence function suited to the task and decoding scheme. It also generalizes lossless speculative decoding by adding a lenience factor in the rejection-sampling step, giving fine-grained control over the quality-vs-latency tradeoff in lossy decoding. A two-step strategy — "distill for performance first, then distill for speculative decoding second" — further reduces latency.
- **Numbers**: ~20% speedup over standard speculative decoding across five tasks, under both greedy decoding and temperature sampling. Up to 8× reduction in latency with minimal performance compromise under the "distill for performance first, distill for speculative decoding second" strategy vs a no-distillation, no-speculative-decoding baseline. (Abstract only; no per-task throughput, token prices, or model sizes given in the available text.)
- **Token-cost implication**: To cut serving latency/cost, pair speculative decoding with knowledge distillation of the draft model toward the target—use on-policy draft data and a task/decoding-appropriate divergence to raise token acceptance; tune the rejection-sampling lenience factor to trade quality for speed, and distill-for-quality before distilling-for-speculation for the largest latency wins.
- **Caveats**: Only the abstract text was available (no methods/results tables, no numeric task-by-task breakdown, no model identities or token-cost figures). Self-reported by Google Research authors. "Lossless" claim applies to the base speculative-decoding step; the lenience factor introduces controlled lossy decoding. The 8× figure is relative to a baseline with neither distillation nor speculative decoding.
