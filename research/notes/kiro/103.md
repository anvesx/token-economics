# 103 — Concise Thoughts: Impact of Output Length on LLM Reasoning and Cost
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19825 (arXiv, 2024-07)
- **Claim**: Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting inflates output length and generation time; a refined prompt strategy, Constrained-CoT (CCoT), that explicitly caps reasoning length produces shorter answers while maintaining or improving accuracy on large models. The paper also introduces three length-aware accuracy metrics (HCA, SCA, CCA).
- **Mechanism**: CCoT appends an explicit length-constraint sentence to the prompt (e.g., "and limit the length of the answer to 30 words"), formalized as x = concat(x_us, x_p, x_l) where x_l states the word limit. Because autoregressive decoders invoke once per output token, fewer output tokens directly reduce generation time. Three metrics re-weight accuracy by length: HCA(k) counts only correct answers ≤ k words; SCA(k,α) adds an exponential-decay tolerance e^((k−N)/α) for over-length answers; CCA(k,α,β) further multiplies by a term penalizing high standard deviation σ of answer lengths. Conciseness is further analyzed via a Redundancy score (syntactic overlap between reasoning steps, Ratcliff/Gestalt matching) and Information Flow (BERTScore semantic similarity between consecutive steps).
- **Numbers**: Using LLaMA2 across GSM8K/SVAMP/ASDIV, CCoT-30 raised average accuracy by 4.41% and cut computation by 5.12s; metric gains of 10%/9%/9% for HCA/SCA/CCA. Example CoT answer: 67 words / 17.64s vs CCoT-45: 34 words / 11.65s (both correct). Llama2-70b GSM8K: generation time 30.09s (CoT) → max 23.86s (CCoT-100); accuracy 36% (CoT) → 37% (CCoT-30) → 41.77% (CCoT-100). Redundancy reduction (ORR): SVAMP 19.77–22.72%, GSM8K 12.64–24.74%. Information Flow reductions for CCoT-15 vs CoT on GSM8K: 26–41%; CCoT-100: 4–20%. Setup: Text Generation Inference on 8× NVIDIA A100; Falcon-40b-instruct and Llama2-70b-chat-hf. GSM8K test >1.3k of 8,000; SVAMP 300; ASDIV 1.22k. Length constraints tested: 15, 30, 45, 60, 100 words.
- **Token-cost implication**: Add an explicit output-length cap to CoT prompts to cut output tokens (and thus latency/cost) with little or no accuracy loss on sufficiently large models; tune the cap to the dataset difficulty (stricter caps like 15 can help simple datasets).
- **Caveats**: Self-reported by the authors; only arithmetic-reasoning datasets tested. Benefit holds for large models (Falcon-40b, Llama2-70b); smaller models (Falcon-7b, Llama2-7b) struggle — they produce higher generation times or incorrect answers under tight caps. LLMs do not always respect the requested length, especially for small limits (15/30/40), so a tolerance margin is needed. Falcon-40b did not improve accuracy on complex GSM8K.
