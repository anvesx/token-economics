# 064 — Pricing Time, Not Just Tokens: Latency-Aware Mechanism Design for LLM Inference
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40098 (arXiv, 2026-09)
- **Claim**: Treats LLM inference as a service market (not a token spot market) where buyers have three-dimensional private types — willingness-to-pay (w), task volume (s), time preference (γ). Its separation theorem shows discrete hardware tiers make time-sensitive buyers self-select into premium tiers, collapsing an intractable 3-D screening problem to standard 1-D screening per tier; optimal per-task prices are volume-independent, theoretically justifying flat per-token API pricing.
- **Mechanism**: Buyer utility is Cobb-Douglas in input/output tokens and latency slack: v = x^α·y^β·(L_max − L)^γ, with α+β+γ<1. Cost derived from GPU inference physics: compute-bound prefill (O(n²) attention, batch-independent) + bandwidth-bound decode (scales with batch size B and context via congestion). Latency fit: L = a·x² + b·x + c + y·(d + e·B + f·B·x). Per-task cost is strictly convex. Separation holds because preferences are log-supermodular in (slack, γ) and premium hardware shows diminishing speedup at large batch — high-γ buyers pick small batches where the premium advantage is largest. Within each tier, optimal quality solves Mussa-Rosen FOC φ(w)=marginal cost; buyers below threshold excluded. Extends to continuous γ via tier-induced partitions. Cost structure C(Q,s)=s·c̃(Q/s) ⇒ no quantity discounts.
- **Numbers**: Separation condition holds in 87/105 configs (82.9%) overall, 67/70 (95.7%) at economically relevant WTP (w≥U[50,100]), 102/105 (97.1%) at single-GPU rates. Two-tier vs best single-tier profit gain 26–66% (specifically at α=0.30,β=0.20: −19.9% at L_max=10s, +26.1% at 30s, +36.8% at 50s, +52.3% at 80s, +65.8% at 100s). Calibrated on Llama3-70B, H100 (economy) vs B200 (premium); cluster rates $18.32/hr (8×H100) vs $30.32/hr (8×B200), ratio 1.66×; single-GPU $2.29/hr vs $3.79/hr (Lambda, 2/9/2026). Decode ratio d_B200/d_H100 = 0.68. Latency fits R²>0.99 (H100 0.9951, B200 0.9967). Hardware specs: H100 80GB HBM3, 3.35 TB/s, 989 TFLOPS FP16; B200 180GB HBM3e, 8 TB/s, 2250 TFLOPS FP16. Price-fluctuation robustness: separation 135/185 checks (73.0%). Referenced $20B Nvidia–Groq licensing deal (2025). 8-GPU tensor-parallel ≈5× faster TTFT vs single GPU.
- **Token-cost implication**: Builders serving latency-sensitive vs batch workloads should offer 2+ hardware tiers (fast/premium vs economy) rather than one — route real-time/interactive traffic to premium low-latency hardware and batch/offline traffic to throughput-optimized economy hardware; this cross-tier allocation captures 26–66% more value precisely when hardware cost is a meaningful fraction of per-request value. Flat per-token pricing (no volume discounts) is theoretically optimal, so don't expect/engineer quantity discounts. maxOutputTokens/latency is a real product axis (e.g. priority tiers, provisioned throughput, "fast mode").
- **Caveats**: Theory paper (EC'26); monopolist model, abstracts from competition, queueing, and capacity. Assumes independence of w, s, γ (rules out correlation). Calibrated to a single hardware pair (H100/B200) with modest decode differential (0.68); gains vanish when the premium tier uniformly dominates (high WTP) or latency budget is tight (L_max=10s). Numerical, self-reported by the authors; LLMs used as adversarial critic/proof-check but text/derivations author-verified.
