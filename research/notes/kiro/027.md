# 027 — Test-Time Speculation

- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09329 (arXiv / University of Texas at Austin, 2026-05)
- **Claim**: Speculative decoding speculators (DFlash, EAGLE-3, PARD) lose their acceptance length as generation gets longer, collapsing toward 1 (no speedup) within a few thousand tokens. Test-Time Speculation (TTS) continuously adapts the draft model at inference using the verification step itself as supervision, recovering acceptance length.
- **Mechanism**: In standard speculative decoding the draft (student) proposes a canvas of C tokens and the target (teacher) verifies them in one forward pass, yielding the target's full distribution over the drafted tokens. TTS treats that already-computed target distribution as an on-policy distillation sample: after each generate-verify round, it takes one gradient step on a distillation loss (position-weighted approximate KL between target and new draft) plus a regularizer keeping the draft near its previous state. The update needs no extra target calls — the verification already produced the signal. One optimization step per round (K=1) is best; more steps overfit. To control overhead, updates can be strided (every S rounds, using only the latest round's signal) and offloaded to a side CUDA stream pipelined over the next S−1 rounds. Implemented on SGLang/DFlash/EAGLE-3; the draft is reset between requests.
- **Numbers**: Acceptance length improves up to 72% and 41% on average (vs DFlash); up to 67% and 34% on average (vs EAGLE-3). EAGLE-3 degrades to ~1.1 acceptance length beyond ~20K tokens. MATH-500: 3.7 (first 10K tokens) → 1.5 (last 10K). Draft cross-entropy on target top-1 token rises 2.8→6.4 nats over generation; at 12K tokens draft assigns ~1/600 to target's top-1. EAGLE-3 Llama3.1-8B drafter trained with 2K max positional encoding, RoPE base 10K vs target 128K / 5×10^5; TTS lifts mean acceptance to 1.96 (61% improvement) for short-context-trained speculators. AIME 2024: +15% in first 10K tokens → +183% in 20–30K range; LiveCodeBench +17% → +245%. Throughput: S=5 gives best mean throughput up to 1.71× over DFlash (Qwen3.5-122B); S=10 gives 1.83× wall-clock on LiveCodeBench/Qwen3-8B. Hardware: 8× NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell (96 GB each). Models 4B–122B across Qwen-3/3.5, Llama3.1; block size 16 (DFlash), depth 8 (EAGLE-3); 32 sequences/benchmark (30 for AIME).
- **Token-cost implication**: For long-output workloads (reasoning, agents, code) don't trust static speculator speedups — they decay with length. Adapting the draft online during a single generation (reusing the verification pass) restores speedup at no extra target-inference cost; tune update stride (S≈5–10) to balance acceptance-length gains against backward-pass overhead.
- **Caveats**: Self-reported by the authors; acceptance length is hardware-independent but throughput depends on the serving stack (SGLang here). Accuracy is unchanged by design (target untouched), so no accuracy metrics reported. Prior online methods (OSD, ATLAS) adapt across requests, not within one generation. Gradient-update kernels are unfused PyTorch (future work); update stride is fixed, not adaptive. Evaluated only on math/science/code long-output benchmarks.
