# 032 — Rethinking Mixture-of-Agents: Is Mixing Different Large Language Models Beneficial?

- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00674 (arXiv / Princeton University, 2025-02)
- **Claim**: Mixture-of-Agents (MoA) ensembling is usually better off aggregating repeated samples from the single strongest model (Self-MoA) than mixing different LLMs, because MoA performance is far more sensitive to proposer quality than to diversity.
- **Mechanism**: MoA queries multiple "proposer" LLMs then uses an "aggregator" LLM to synthesize their outputs. Self-MoA keeps the same prompting template but samples the single best-performing model repeatedly (in-model diversity) instead of mixing models (cross-model diversity), raising average proposer quality. Self-MoA-Seq extends this to arbitrary sample counts via a sliding window: aggregate a fixed number of responses at a time, repeat the current synthesized output in the first window slots to bias the aggregator toward it, reducing context-length requirements. Diversity measured by Vendi Score (effective number of unique outputs); quality = average individual proposer accuracy. A linear regression t = αq + βd + γ fits MoA performance on quality q and diversity d.
- **Numbers**: Self-MoA beats mixed MoA by 6.6 points on AlpacaEval 2.0 LC win rate (65.7 vs 59.1, both with Qwen1.5-110B-Chat aggregator, 6 proposers), and by 3.8% average across MMLU/CRUX/MATH. Applied to top models: Self-MoA + gemma-2-9b-it-WPO-HB 78.5 vs 76.7 individual; + SimPO 75.0 vs 72.4 (2–3 pt gain, SOTA on leaderboard, only 4 outputs due to context). Regression R² ≈ 0.7 (0.771 MMLU, 0.685 CRUX, 0.760 MATH); α > β on all three datasets (quality matters more). Centered-1/K-Norm with K=2 raises MMLU R² to 0.9+. Over 200 experiments / 70 proposer mixtures. When Mixed-MoA wins (constructed mixture task), it beats Self-MoA(dddddd) by only 0.17% (mmdddd 59.86%) and 0.35% (mmmddd 60.04%). 3-Layer Mixed-MoA 65.4 LC with 13 forward passes vs 2-Layer Self-MoA 65.7 with 7 passes. Adding Llama-3.1-8B to Qwen2 Self-MoA-Seq +~2%; adding weaker DeepSeek-Coder −1.5%. Temperature 0.7 proposers, 0 aggregator.
- **Token-cost implication**: For ensemble/aggregation inference, prefer repeated samples from one strong model over mixing many models — it is both cheaper (half the forward passes to match 3-layer mixed MoA) and higher quality. Only mix models when their individual qualities are close AND cross-model diversity is high. Use Self-MoA-Seq to ensemble many samples under short context limits without extra quality loss.
- **Caveats**: Self-reported (Princeton). GPT-4-based AlpacaEval annotator (LC win rate to debias length). Benefit of Self-MoA is strongest when proposer quality varies; a poor choice of proposer model hurts (their single-proposer result differs from original MoA paper, attributed to proposer choice). Mixed-MoA can slightly win on constructed multi-domain mixtures. Diminishing returns as samples grow; no universal compute-optimal sample count.
