# 119 — LaRA: Benchmarking Retrieval-Augmented Generation and Long-Context LLMs - No Silver Bullet for LC or RAG Routing
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09977 (HKUST / Tongyi Lab, Alibaba / Penn State, Li et al., 2025-02; v2 Mar 2025)
- **Claim**: A new benchmark of 2326 test cases shows no universal winner between RAG and feeding the full long context (LC): the better choice depends on model size and long-text ability, context length, task type, and chunk settings.
- **Mechanism**: Benchmark design: natural long texts (novels, financial statements, arXiv papers) at about 32k and 128k tokens; novels get GPT-4o entity replacement to limit leakage; four task types (location, reasoning, comparison, hallucination detection); QA built from human seeds plus LLM generation; GPT-4o judge validated by Cohen's Kappa against humans. RAG setup: 600-token chunks, 100-token overlap, 5 chunks retrieved, GTE-large-en-v1.5 embeddings plus BM25 hybrid search. 11 LLMs tested (7 open-source, 4 proprietary). The paper frames the results as inputs to a RAG-or-LC routing workflow.
- **Numbers**: At 32k, LC beat RAG by 2.4% average accuracy across all models; at 128k RAG beat LC by 3.68%. At 128k RAG beat LC by 6.48% (Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct) and 38.12% (Mistral-Nemo-12B). GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet LC beat RAG on reasoning at 128k by 9.09% and 8.98%. Comparison tasks: LC ahead of RAG by an average gap of 15.22% (32k) and 14.30% (128k). Location tasks: average gap 0.08% (32k) and -5.25% (128k, favouring RAG). RAG wins hallucination detection; GPT-4o LC scores 56.34% there. GPT-4o and Qwen-2.5-72B beat their smaller siblings by 7.35% to 16.2% in LC. Qwen-2.5-7B at 128k gained 7.39% with RAG, Llama-3.1-8B 0.15%. Larger chunk count helps the 72B model steadily; the 7B model peaks at an intermediate count. LC accuracy drops when the answer sits mid-context; RAG shows no clear position effect. Cost is only discussed qualitatively; no dollar or token figures are given. Full table values were garbled in extraction; figures above are from prose.
- **Token-cost implication**: Do not default to stuffing the whole context. Route by model and task: for small or weaker models and contexts near 128k, retrieval gives equal or better accuracy on far fewer input tokens; for strong frontier models, LC wins on reasoning and cross-passage comparison. Use RAG for lookup and hallucination-sensitive questions, LC (or more retrieved chunks) for comparison and reasoning. For a small model, tune chunk count since too many chunks adds noise.
- **Caveats**: Authors' own benchmark with GPT-4o as judge (and GPT-4o also used to build data, a possible bias). One fixed RAG configuration for the main table (600-token chunks, 5 chunks, hybrid retrieval), so RAG results depend on that retriever. Only two context lengths, three document types, models from late 2024. Does not measure latency or price, so the cost argument is inferred from token counts rather than measured. The "weaker model" finding is confounded with differing long-context training.
