# 036 — Toward Inference-optimal Mixture-of-Expert Large Language Models
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02852 (UC San Diego, CMU, Univ. of Edinburgh; arXiv, 2024-04)
- **Claim**: Extends MoE scaling laws to include number of experts and adds inference cost per token as a constraint; finds few-expert (4/8) MoEs are cheapest to serve at equal quality but cost far more to train, while smaller over-trained 8/16-expert MoEs match their quality at about half the inference cost.
- **Mechanism**: Fits a scaling law log L(N,D,E) over dense sizes 100M-730M with 4/8/16/32 experts trained on 2.5B-20B SlimPajama tokens (Top-2 gating, MoE every other layer). Inference cost = G x C0 / throughput, throughput from KV-cache-limited max batch size (GPU memory minus parameters, divided by per-request KV size) and profiled prompt/decode latency on 8x40GB A100 with vLLM; MoE size = (1+(E-1)/3) x dense size N. "Over-training" shrinks the model below the loss-optimal size and spends the savings on more tokens, searched by dichotomy against a loss or inference-cost bound.
- **Numbers**: 4/8-expert MoEs most serving-efficient at equal performance but need 2.4x-4.3x (abstract: 2.5-3.5x) more training budget. A 16-expert MoE needs 23.7%-42.8% of the FLOPs of a 4-expert MoE for the same quality. Over-trained 8- or 16-expert MoE matching a loss-optimal 4-expert MoE needs 47.0%-52.0% of its inference cost; at equal inference cost an over-trained 16-expert saves up to 68.4% of training budget. Matching a dense model: over-trained 8-expert costs 31.6%-38.1% of dense inference cost (budgets 5.15e21-8.18e21 FLOPs). Recommended: 32-expert at 30% of loss-optimal size (quality focus) or 16-expert at 16% (cost focus). MFU varies 10x with batch size.
- **Token-cost implication**: Serving cost per token scales roughly linearly with total parameters because more experts squeeze KV-cache memory and batch size; if you pick or train MoE models for high-volume serving, favor smaller, longer-trained models over loss-optimal giant expert counts.
- **Caveats**: Academic, small-scale models (up to 730M dense), 20B tokens max, extrapolated by fitted law; cost estimated on A100 with a simple latency interpolation model, not real production traffic; ShareGPT used for prompt/output lengths.
