# 123 — Economic Evaluation of LLMs
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03834 (California Institute of Technology, Zellinger and Thomson, 2025-07; preprint under review)
- **Claim**: Replaces accuracy-cost Pareto plots with a single dollar-denominated score per model, built from the price of an error, the price of latency and the price of abstention. On hard MATH questions it concludes that reasoning models and single large models usually beat cheaper alternatives once errors cost more than a few cents to dollars.
- **Mechanism**: Per-query reward r = -(C + lambda_L * L + lambda_E * 1[error] + lambda_A * 1[abstain]), where C = N_in * C_in + N_out * C_out at API prices, L is measured wall-clock latency, and lambda values are dollar "shadow prices". Choose the model or system configuration that maximises expected reward; sweep lambda to get sensitivity maps. Cascades pay cost and latency of every model up to the one that answers; routers add a small routing overhead (router model under 1B parameters). Cascade quality is predicted by "cascade error reduction" CER = Cov(deferral, small-model error), with the small model's confidence from self-verification P(True). Theorem 1 shows sweeping lambda recovers the Pareto surface. Price-of-error example: medical diagnosis about $333 (lambda_E = E[cost|lawsuit] x P(E|M)P(M)/P(E), with $500,000 payout, 2/3 genuine error, 1/100,000 and 1/100 event rates), rounded to about $1,000 in text.
- **Numbers**: Setup: MATH training split, non-numeric answers filtered, 500 questions per difficulty level 1, 3, 5; 6 models (Llama3.3 70B, Llama3.1 405B, GPT-4.1, o3, DeepSeek R1, Qwen3 235B-A22B); zero-shot chain of thought; API calls via Fireworks AI and OpenAI; Llama3.1 405B as grader. On level 5: reasoning models have much lower error rates but 10-100x the cost per query and up to 10x the latency; output-token growth with difficulty is similar for reasoning and non-reasoning models, but reasoning has a higher baseline. Reasoning wins on accuracy-cost (no latency price) once price of error exceeds $0.20 (Figure 3 and body text; the abstract and conclusion say $0.01, an internal inconsistency in the paper). With latency priced, reasoning wins above $10 error price at up to $5/min latency, and above $100 at $10/min ($600/hour wage equivalent). Qwen3-235B-A22B, o3 and Llama3.3 70B are the preferred models in most scenarios. Single big model (Qwen3 235B-A22B) beats cascades above $0.10 price of error with zero latency price; critical price rises to $10 at $0.5/min and $1,000 at $10/min. Exception: the Llama3.1 405B to Qwen3 cascade beats Qwen3 alone up to $10,000 error price and $10/min latency, although Llama3.1 405B alone is worse than Llama3.3 70B; attributed to its strong self-verification (CER). Cascade tuned on 250 questions, evaluated on 250. API round-trip latency under 300 ms.
- **Token-cost implication**: Do not minimise token cost in isolation. Put a dollar value on an error and on delay, then compute expected reward per model. For tasks where an error costs more than about $0.10 to $10, a strong (often reasoning) model beats cheap models or cascades despite 10-100x token spend. Before building a cascade, measure how well the small model's confidence separates its right and wrong answers (CER); a poor confidence signal makes the cascade lose to the big model alone.
- **Caveats**: Academic, self-run study on one benchmark (MATH, hard problems, training split so contamination is possible). Grader is Llama3.1 405B, which may flatter that model's self-verification (authors say the effect is limited). Extrapolation to other domains is the authors' own claim. Dollar prices of error and latency are assumed, not measured; the medical estimate uses rough rates. Latency excludes high-throughput or web-serving use cases. Prices are those of mid-2025 API pricing at test time. The $0.01 vs $0.20 critical-error-price discrepancy is unresolved in the text. Figure values were garbled in extraction.
