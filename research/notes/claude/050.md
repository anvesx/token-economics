# 050 — Holistic Evaluation of Language Models
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09110 (Stanford CRFM, 2022-11)
- **Claim**: HELM evaluates 30 language models across 42 scenarios on 7 metrics (accuracy, calibration, robustness, fairness, bias, toxicity, efficiency), so that trade-offs beyond accuracy are visible under one standardised setup.
- **Mechanism**: A taxonomy of scenarios and desiderata, then a chosen subset: 16 core scenarios, with all 7 metrics measured on them where possible (87.5% of the time), plus 7 targeted evaluations for skills and risks. All models run under the same prompting and adaptation (5-shot in the headline setup), and all raw prompts and completions are released. Efficiency is measured as training and inference cost that depends on model architecture and hardware.
- **Numbers**: Before HELM, models were evaluated on average on 17.9% of the core scenarios; HELM raises coverage to 96.0% (all 30 models densely benchmarked on the core set). Finding 7: no strong accuracy-efficiency trade-off across the 30 models; within a family (e.g. GPT-3 sizes) larger models are more accurate with higher training and inference cost; only a subset of models sit on the accuracy-efficiency Pareto frontier for each scenario. Instruction-tuned models (Anthropic-LM v4-s3 52B and text-davinci-002) rank top 3 on accuracy, robustness and fairness, despite being more than 10x smaller than TNLG v2 (530B). Prompt-format sensitivity: OPT (175B) on HellaSwag scores 79.1% with each choice in a separate 0-shot prompt and 30.2% with choices in one 5-shot multiple-choice prompt. text-davinci-002 scores 40.8% on the RAFT Systematic Review Inclusion split versus 97.5% for several models. No prices.
- **Token-cost implication**: Do not assume a bigger or pricier model is the only way to better quality: instruction-tuned smaller models can sit on the accuracy-efficiency frontier. Choose models by measuring accuracy and cost together on your own prompts, and keep prompt format fixed when comparing because format alone can move accuracy by tens of points.
- **Caveats**: Stanford authors' own benchmark; models are from 2022 (GPT-3 era, text-davinci-002, OPT, BLOOM), so rankings are obsolete. The paper states the open versus closed gap is a snapshot, and that PaLM and Gopher were not evaluated. Efficiency numbers depend on hardware and implementation and are not a price per token. Standardised prompts may disadvantage some models.
