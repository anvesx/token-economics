# 082 — Learning to Compress Prompts with Gist Tokens
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08467 (arXiv; Stanford University, NeurIPS 2023, 2023-04)
- **Claim**: Gisting trains an LM to compress a prompt into one or a few "gist" tokens whose key/value activations can be cached and reused. It yields up to 26x prompt compression with a small quality drop and a 40% FLOPs reduction, at no extra training cost beyond instruction tuning.
- **Mechanism**: During instruction tuning, k copies of a new gist token are inserted between the task prompt t and the input x: (t, g1..gk, x). The attention mask is modified so that tokens after the gist tokens cannot attend to prompt tokens before them (decoder-only: mask out the lower-left corner; encoder-decoder: also mask encoder and cross-attention). This forces the model to compress t into the gist activations. At inference, G(t) is predicted zero-shot for any new prompt, cached as a prefix and reused. About 10 lines of PyTorch. Trained on Alpaca+ (130,321 examples, 104,664 unique tasks) on LLaMA-7B and FLAN-T5-XXL (11B), with k in {1, 2, 5, 10}; models were insensitive to k, so single-token models were used.
- **Numbers**: Single gist token, ChatGPT win rate against the uncompressed positive control: LLaMA-7B 48.6% Seen, 49.7% Unseen, 45.8% Human (OOD); FLAN-T5-XXL 50.8%, 46.2%, 42.5%. ROUGE-L LLaMA-7B Human split: positive control 27.0, gist 23.9, TF-IDF 16.5, negative control 14.4. Human eval (100 examples, 3 annotators): gist win rate 52.3% for LLaMA-7B and 40.6% for FLAN-T5-XXL. Compression is 26x on average (average prompt about 26 tokens down to 1 token). Exact output match with the positive control: about 50% on Seen tasks, 20-25% on Unseen, about 10% on Human. Efficiency (single forward pass, 252 Human instructions): LLaMA-7B CUDA time 23.4 ms none, 22.1 ms instruction cache, 21.8 ms gist; GFLOPs 915 none, 553 instruction cache, 552 gist. Gist vs no caching: 40% FLOPs saving (95% CI 37-42) and 6.8% wall time for LLaMA-7B; FLAN-T5-XXL 40% FLOPs and 4.2% wall time (31.0 to 29.7 ms). Gist vs instruction caching: 0.11% FLOPs and 1.0% wall time. KV cache is 1.05 MB per token for LLaMA-7B (fp32), so gisting allows caching up to 26x more prompts in the same storage.
- **Token-cost implication**: Gisting only pays off when you control the model weights (self-hosted) and reuse the same long prompt across many requests; you must retrain or fine-tune with the gist mask. For API users it does not apply. Compare against ordinary prefix KV caching: its compute gain over gisting is tiny here, and the real win is storage and context-window space.
- **Caveats**: Authors' own results at small scale: 7B and 11B models, short prompts (about 20-26 tokens), Alpaca+ data from GPT-3 variants, ChatGPT-3.5 as judge (human eval only 100 examples, low Cohen's kappa .24-.33). Failure cases include lost specifics (verbatim category names) and runaway generations. Wall-time gains are small at this scale and larger gains were not measured on long prompts. Needs finetuning, no use on closed models.
