# 001 — RouteLLM: Learning to Route LLMs with Preference Data
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665 (LMSYS / UC Berkeley, Anyscale, Canva; 2024-06, ICLR 2025 version)
- **Claim**: Routers trained on Chatbot Arena human preference data (plus augmentation) choose per query between one strong and one weak LLM, cutting strong-model calls by up to ~3.66x cost savings on MT Bench at 95% of GPT-4 quality. Routers transfer to unseen model pairs without retraining.
- **Mechanism**: Binary router R^alpha(q) = weak model if P(strong wins | q) < alpha, else strong model; alpha is the cost threshold. P(win) is learned from preference labels (strong wins / tie / weak wins) with four parameterizations: similarity-weighted Bradley-Terry ranking (embedding cosine similarity with exponential weights, gamma=10, solved at inference, no training), matrix factorization (bilinear score of model embedding and query embedding, trained on 8GB GPU, ~10 epochs), BERT-base classifier (full fine-tune, 2xL4, ~2000 steps), and Llama 3 8B causal LLM classifier (8xA100, ~2000 steps). Arena models are clustered into 10 tiers (tiers 0-1 strong, tier 2 weak) to reduce label sparsity. Data augmentation: golden-labeled MMLU validation (~1500 questions) and GPT-4-judge labels on Nectar queries (~120K samples). Embeddings: OpenAI text-embedding-3-small. Metrics: CPT(x%) = minimum share of strong calls to reach x% performance-gap recovered; APGR = average PGR across call shares.
- **Numbers**: Train data 80K Arena battles (65k after pruning, 64 models). Strong gpt-4-1106-preview, weak Mixtral 8x7B. MT Bench, Arena+judge data: matrix factorization CPT(50%)=13.40%, CPT(80%)=31.31% vs random 49.03% / 78.08%; APGR 0.802 vs 0.500. MT Bench score at CPT(50%) is 8.8, 95% of GPT-4's 9.3. MMLU (5-shot) with Arena+gold: CPT(50%) about 35.4-41.3% vs random 50.07%; score 75 = 92% of GPT-4's 81. GSM8K (8-shot) with Arena+judge: causal LLM CPT(50%)=33.64% vs random 50.00%; score 75 = 87% of GPT-4's 86. Cost saving ratio vs random: MT Bench 3.66x (CPT50) / 2.49x (CPT80); MMLU 1.41x / 1.14x; GSM8K 1.49x / 1.27x. Cost basis: GPT-4 $24.7 per 1M tokens (gpt-4-1106 at $10 in / $30 out per 1M, 95 avg input and 264 avg output tokens); Mixtral $0.24 per 1M tokens. Judge-label data cost about $700. Router overhead: SW ranking $39.26 per million requests (2.9 req/s, $0.39/hr CPU VM); matrix factorization $3.32 per million (155.16 req/s, $0.8/hr L4 VM); BERT $3.19 (69.62 req/s); causal LLM $5.23 (42.46 req/s); max overhead <=0.4% of GPT-4 generation cost. Transfer, MT Bench, no retraining: Claude 3 Opus/Sonnet best APGR 0.772 vs random 0.493; Llama 3.1 70B/8B best 0.767 vs 0.512. Versus commercial routers (Unify AI, Martian) the best routers match performance with up to 40% fewer GPT-4 calls. Footnote: Haiku $1.25 vs Opus $75 per 1M output tokens (over 50x).
- **Token-cost implication**: A learned binary router in front of a strong/weak model pair can halve strong-model spend on chat-like traffic; the router itself costs well under 1% of strong-model cost. Collect a small amount of in-domain labeled data (even ~1500 samples, <2% of training data) because Arena-only routers performed near random on MMLU and GSM8K.
- **Caveats**: Academic benchmarks (MT Bench, MMLU, GSM8K), two-model only; savings are relative to a random router at equal call share, not to an always-weak baseline. Savings vary sharply by benchmark (3.66x vs 1.14x-1.49x). Arena-only training failed out-of-distribution (BERT and others near or below random). Authors' own experiments; cost model assumes short single-turn prompts (95 in / 264 out tokens) and gpt-4-1106 pricing of the time.
- **Topic**: routing
