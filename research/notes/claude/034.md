# 034 — DeepSeekMoE: Towards Ultimate Expert Specialization in Mixture-of-Experts Language Models
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06066 (DeepSeek AI with Peking University, 2024-01)
- **Claim**: An MoE design with many small routed experts plus always-on shared experts reaches the quality of dense or larger-compute MoE models with far fewer activated parameters and FLOPs. DeepSeekMoE 16B matches LLaMA2 7B at 39.6% of its compute.
- **Mechanism**: Two changes to a standard top-K MoE layer. (1) Fine-grained segmentation: split each expert FFN into m smaller experts (hidden size 1/m) and activate m*K of them, so FLOPs and parameters stay constant while expert combinations grow (N=16, top-2 gives 120 combinations; m=4 gives C(64,8) = 4,426,165,368). (2) Shared expert isolation: Ks experts bypass the router and process every token, and the router picks mK - Ks of the rest, which reduces redundancy among routed experts. Load balance uses an expert-level balance loss (factor 0.01 in small runs, 0.003 at 145B) and a device-level balance loss (0.05 at 145B). Validation model: 1 shared plus 63 routed experts, each 0.25 of a standard FFN.
- **Numbers**: Validation runs on 100B training tokens, 2B total and about 0.3B activated parameters: DeepSeekMoE matches GShard 2.9B (1.5x expert parameters and compute) and approaches a dense model with the same total parameters. DeepSeekMoE 16B: 16.4B total, 2.8B activated, 74.4T FLOPs per 4K tokens, trained on 2T tokens, versus DeepSeek 7B dense (6.9B, 183.5T FLOPs per 4K tokens) and LLaMA2 7B (6.7B, 187.9T FLOPs per 4K tokens). That is 39.6% of LLaMA2 7B's computation with 245% of its total parameters, and about 40% of DeepSeek 7B's. Fits on one 40GB GPU without quantization; paper says "nearly 2.5 times the inference speed of a 7B dense model" with operator optimisation (no hardware or batch detail given). DeepSeekMoE 145B matches DeepSeek 67B dense at 28.5% of computation; the 142B half-activated variant (2 shared, 6 of 128 routed active) does so at 18.2%. Weakness: lags on multiple-choice tasks such as MMLU, attributed to about 0.5B attention parameters in the 16B model versus 2.5B in DeepSeek 7B.
- **Token-cost implication**: Per-token serving cost tracks activated parameters, not total parameters. When choosing between dense and MoE models for the same quality, compare activated parameters and FLOPs per token, while still budgeting GPU memory for all experts. Expect cheaper generation and knowledge tasks but check multiple-choice and reasoning quality yourself.
- **Caveats**: Authors' own training study, self-reported, mostly at 2B to 16B with a preliminary 145B run. Compute is measured in FLOPs, not dollars or measured throughput; the 2.5x speed claim is a single sentence without a setup. No pricing data. Quality comparisons are on the authors' benchmark mix with a Chinese and English corpus.
