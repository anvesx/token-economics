# 094 — Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters
- **Source**: arXiv 2408.03314 (Google DeepMind, Snell et al., 2024-08)
- **Claim**: Test-time compute should be allocated per prompt by difficulty, not uniformly. A "compute-optimal" policy beats best-of-N with 2-4x less test-time compute, and on easy and medium questions a smaller model with test-time compute can beat a model with about 14x more parameters in a FLOPs-matched comparison.
- **Mechanism**: Two axes: (1) search against a process reward model (PRM) verifier: best-of-N weighted, beam search (beam width M=4), lookahead search (k-step rollout; cost counted as N x (k+1) samples); (2) a model fine-tuned to revise its own answers sequentially (trained on up to 4 incorrect in-context answers paired with a correct one, built from 64 parallel samples, matched by character edit distance). Compute-optimal policy: estimate difficulty as 5 quantile bins of pass@1 (2048 samples; oracle from ground truth, or predicted from PRM scores), then pick the method and sequential/parallel ratio best for each bin and budget. Selection among revisions uses a verifier or majority vote, because about 38% of correct answers get turned into incorrect ones by naive revision. Exchange rate with pretraining: X = 6 N D_pretrain, Y = 2 N D_inference; compare at R = D_inference / D_pretrain.
- **Numbers**: PaLM 2-S* (Codey) on MATH (500-question test split), budgets up to 256 generations. Compute-optimal search roughly matches best-of-N at 4x less compute (16 vs 64 generations). Compute-optimal revisions beat best-of-N with up to 4x less compute (64 vs 256 samples). Discussion states a 2-4x efficiency gain. FLOPs-matched comparison against a model with about 14x more parameters at R = 0.16, 0.79 and 22. Beam search beats best-of-N at small budgets and on difficulty levels 3-4; on levels 1-2 it degrades with more budget; level 5 shows little progress from any method. Easy questions do best with fully sequential revisions; hard questions need a mix.
- **Token-cost implication**: Do not use a fixed N or fixed thinking budget for every request. Route by estimated difficulty: spend little (or revisions only) on easy prompts, parallel samples plus search on medium-hard prompts, and avoid spending on prompts the model cannot solve. If production inference volume is much larger than pretraining volume (R >> 1) or the questions are hard, a larger model is the cheaper way to get accuracy.
- **Caveats**: Self-reported by the authors, MATH only, one model family (PaLM 2-S*). The compute to estimate difficulty is not counted in the savings. The PRM and revision models need task-specific fine-tuning (PRM trained with Monte Carlo rollouts since PRM800k was ineffective). Search over-optimizes the PRM at high budgets. Pretraining comparison only scales parameters with fixed data. Did not test combining PRM tree search with revisions or critique-and-revise. Figure data is garbled in the text extraction, so only figures stated in prose are cited.
