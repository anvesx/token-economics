# 007 — Efficient Training-Free Online Routing for High-Volume Multi-LLM Serving
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02718 (arXiv, University of Wisconsin-Madison, NeurIPS 2025, 2025-09; v3 12 Dec 2025)
- **Claim**: PORT is a training-free online router for serving many queries across several LLMs under fixed per-model token budgets. It estimates per-model quality and cost by approximate-nearest-neighbour lookup on historical queries and learns routing weights once from a small initial sample.
- **Mechanism**: Offline problem is a MILP maximising total quality subject to per-model token budgets (cost g = prefill price*input length + decode price*output length). Per query, quality d and cost g for every model are estimated as the mean over 5 nearest neighbours (HNSW/DiskANN) in a historical set of (query, response, score, cost) records, embedded with bge-base-en-v1.5. LP relaxation gives a dual with one weight gamma_i per LLM. The first eps fraction of queries (eps = 0.025, about 250 queries) is routed at random to learn gamma* by minimising the dual; every later query goes to argmax_i (alpha*d_ij - gamma_i*g_ij) with alpha = 0.0001, queued if that model's budget is spent. No model training; changing the model set only means updating the historical dataset. Theorem: competitive ratio 1 - O(eps + delta) vs offline optimum under stated assumptions.
- **Numbers**: Abstract: average 3.55x performance, 1.85x cost efficiency, nearly 4.25x throughput over 8 baselines on 3 benchmarks (RouterBench with 11 LLMs, SPROUT with 13, Open LLM Leaderboard v2 with 18). Vs strongest baseline BatchSplit (batch 256): +33% performance, +38% cost efficiency, +24% throughput. Reaches 75.99% to 84.66% of the offline approximate optimum. Table 1 (budget = cost of the cheapest single model): RouterBench perf 2718.6 vs BatchSplit 1838.05, throughput 5195 vs 3903; SPROUT perf 4513.05 vs 3975.5; Open LLM v2 perf 1465.0 vs 1059.0. Gap to BatchSplit grows with load, about 50% higher performance on RouterBench at maximum volume; with 80% of budget on one model (h=1) about 2x BatchSplit. Relaxed LP vs MILP gap 0.016% on SPROUT. Test sets 10,000 queries (RouterBench, Open LLM v2); volume swept 4,000 to 12,000; budgets swept 0.25x to 2x; history swept 5,000 to 25,000; neighbours 3 to 10.
- **Token-cost implication**: If you run several models with separate token or rate budgets, route by a per-model price weight (gamma) rather than by predicted quality alone. A nearest-neighbour index over logged (query, model, score, cost) rows is enough, with no router training, and adding or removing a model only changes the index. Keep roughly 250 initial queries randomly routed to calibrate weights.
- **Caveats**: Authors' own experiments on offline benchmark logs, not a production deployment. Needs ground-truth scores and costs for historical queries. Assumes random arrival order and embedding proximity implying similar quality; operational state such as live traffic or load is out of scope. Cost is a token budget abstraction, not dollar billing, and caching effects are not modelled. Router latency is reported as negligible in an appendix table that was not in the readable text.
- **Topic**: routing
